1923

A horse, a plough and two lawns

Mr F. Stimpson, Chairman of Stephens Shire Council, obtained the north east corner of Yeronga Park for croquet lawns. On 15 October he arrived with horse and plough to prepare the ground. Two lawns were laid and a small clubhouse was erected.

The club commenced with a membership of 18, and Mr Stimpson's wife became the first president. The inaugural members were ladies of the Stephens Shire, taught the game by members of East Brisbane Croquet Club.

A vintage croquet set: mallets and wooden balls (stock photo)

1930s – 1940s

Depression, debt and wartime tea

The Great Depression had its effect on Stephens, and by the end of the 1930s the club was still in debt to its founders. Membership was capped at 41, and a septic tank was installed. By 1939 membership was recorded at 94.

Wartime brought stringent cost cutting. Money was tight and the club was still in debt, so it was decided to stop paying the tea lady, and members needed to bring their own afternoon tea.

1950s – 1960s

Curtains, cardigans and the men question

Croquet continued to flourish through the 1950s, and clubhouse additions such as curtains, new paint and shed roofs made postwar life more enjoyable. The wearing of dark coloured cardigans was banned.

In 1961 there was discussion about accepting men into the club, but the matter was deferred. Men had previously been employed only as greenkeepers. They were finally admitted as players some time before 1976.

Wooden croquet mallets bundled in a clubroom (stock photo)

1980s

A reputation for the best lawns

This decade seemed to be a very important and successful one for Stephens. Many championships were played, and QCA and ACC events hosted, with the club gaining a reputation for the high quality of its lawns.

In 1987 Golf Croquet was mentioned for the first time, and in 1988 the clubhouse was repainted inside and out at a cost of $2,116.

Mallets, balls and a hoop set up on the lawn (stock photo)

1990s – 2000s

New codes, new rules

In 1995 the dress code was made more specific: additional colour to be only royal blue, hat to be white with a Stephens hatband, and common rules of decency and decorum to be observed.

By 2005 there was mention of Ricochet and Gateball, with Golf Croquet numbers growing, and in 2006 Golf Croquet players were given full membership after much contention.

2010s

A refurbished kitchen and a century of friendships

The clubhouse kitchen was refurbished in 2010 after successful grant applications. By 2013, ninety years after beginning as a fashionable game for prewar ladies, Stephens had a membership of 41: a dynamic mix of social and competitive players, several of national and international standard.

The Stephens clubhouse, set up for a function

Today

Yeronga Park, still on the lawns

Croquet is now a sport for the 21st century, played by people of all ages and abilities in the beautiful setting of Yeronga Memorial Park.

Stephens still fields 4 full size courts, lit for evening play, with Association Croquet on Wednesday mornings and Saturday afternoons, and Golf Croquet on Friday and Sunday afternoons.
